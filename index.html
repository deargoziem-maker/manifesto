    Why If Na You exists

    There is a sentence I have been arguing with for years:

    Nigerian politics isn't driven by ideology.

    Usually, it is offered as the end of the conversation. I think it is where a more interesting one begins.

    If you wanted to raise the quality of Nigerian political argument over twenty years, I don't think you would start with candidates, parties or manifestos.

    You would start with language.

    People cannot hold anyone to a position they have no words for. And it is difficult to understand what we disagree about when our political vocabulary doesn't describe the choices we are actually making.

    So the question behind If Na You is:

    What would a Nigerian political spectrum look like if we derived it from our own history rather than importing left and right?

    If Na You is one attempt at an answer.

    It isn't designed to tell you what to believe or who to vote for. It is a way to examine your own political instincts, confront the trade-offs behind them, and understand where they sit within Nigeria's political traditions.

    The framework is an argument, not a discovery. The methodology is public so you can examine it, challenge it, and decide for yourself where it gets things wrong.
