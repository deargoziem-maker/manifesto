Start with the demand and the supply has to follow. That begins with you knowing your own position well enough to notice when nobody is offering it.

Methodology · v0.2

How a position is calculated

The method is published so it can be contested. Anyone disputing a result should be able to reconstruct it by hand.

1. Seven dimensions, chosen because Nigerians disagree about them

Nigerian politics does not fit a single left–right line, and it does not fit the imported authoritarian–libertarian square. Each dimension below names an argument that is live and unresolved.

2. Weighted statements, not archetype counting

Each statement carries signed loadings on one dimension. Agreement is recorded from −2 to +2, multiplied by the loading and summed; the total is divided by the maximum possible magnitude for that dimension, producing a score from −100 to +100.

Why not count points per archetype? Because the result would then depend on how many statements happen to mention each archetype. Add three more restructuring questions and everyone becomes a Restructurer. Normalising each dimension separately removes that artefact.

Loadings on each dimension sum to exactly zero. A respondent who agrees with all twenty-one statements, or disagrees with all of them, lands precisely at the origin rather than drifting toward a pole. This removes acquiescence bias, which would otherwise be read as a political position. The anchor item alternates between poles across dimensions so the instrument is not systematically keyed in one direction.

3. Archetypes as regions, not bins

Each of the ten archetypes is a fixed point in the seven-dimensional space. Your position is compared to all ten by Euclidean distance; the nearest is your primary reading. The distribution shown is a distance-weighted soft assignment, exp(−d / 50) normalised across the ten, so a position between two traditions is reported as between them.

The archetype set was tested by constructing, for each archetype, a respondent answering in full accordance with it. All ten recover their own archetype, and the minimum distance between any two archetypes is 87.7 units on a scale where the maximum possible separation is 529.

4. Calibration against a probability sample

The methodological literature on voting advice applications is blunt on this point: without calibration against real survey data, these instruments produce invalid results. Part 2 therefore draws seven items directly from Afrobarometer's Round 10 Nigeria instrument, fielded to 1,600 adults on a random stratified probability sample in June–July 2024, with a margin of error of ±2.5 points.

Those items come with known national distributions, so your answer is reported against what Nigerians actually said rather than against nothing.

What calibration revealed. Most "good governance" attitudes in Nigeria are near-consensus and therefore useless for placing anyone: 89% want a two-term limit, 90% reject one-party rule, 86% want officials to do what voters demand, 80% want presidential spending oversight. An instrument built on those items would put everyone in the same place.

The real variance sits in regime commitment. Only 64% hold that democracy is always preferable. 27% would approve of the army governing. And 54% — a majority — say it is legitimate for the armed forces to seize power from leaders who abuse it. The same population that overwhelmingly demands term limits and oversight also, by majority, accepts a coup against an abusive government. That is not incoherence; it is a commitment to democratic procedure that is conditional on outcomes, and it is the most interesting thing in the Nigerian data.

5. What is deliberately excluded

  • No personality typing affects scoring. A temperament label appears because people enjoy it. It contributes nothing and no claim is made that it predicts political behaviour.
  • No living politician is used as a match. Matching a citizen to a serving or campaigning politician is an endorsement mechanism wearing the costume of a quiz. Living figures appear only in the , on documented positions.
  • No ethnic, religious or state-of-origin variable enters the score. The instrument would become an ethnic classifier if it did.

6. Known limitations

  • The dimensions are asserted, not discovered. A validated instrument would field a pool of sixty to eighty items on a pilot sample of two to three thousand Nigerians, then use factor analysis or item-response theory to find how many dimensions exist and what loads on them. That work has not been done. The item weights here are editorial judgements, not estimated parameters.
  • Regional confounding is unmeasured and dangerous. Items on religion in law and on traditional authority correlate with region in Nigeria. Left untested, the instrument could reliably output "Northerners are Traditionalists" and confirm the exact essentialism it exists to defeat. The pilot must test every item for regional confounding and publish the confound where it cannot be removed.
  • Twenty-one statements cannot separate positions that differ subtly. Treat the reading as the start of an argument, not a verdict.
  • Self-selected respondents are not Nigeria. Aggregate figures from this tool would describe who took it, which is why the benchmarks come from a probability sample instead.

7. Data handling

This prototype collects nothing. No account, no identifier, no server; every calculation happens in your browser and closing the tab destroys the result. A production deployment would add an anonymous aggregate store holding seven integers per completed response, with no key linking any record to a person.

Political opinion is sensitive personal data under the Nigeria Data Protection Act. A production deployment requires a plain-language privacy notice, registration with the Nigeria Data Protection Commission where applicable, and independent methodological review before any electoral period.

The Register · editorial protocol

Putting the people who seek your vote on the record

This is the one place where living, serving and campaigning figures appear. It is built to a different standard from the rest of the platform, because the risk is different and because this is where the platform earns its name.

The mechanism

The directory is published before the first invitation goes out. Every candidate on INEC's final list appears in the from launch, beside every rival in their own race, with one column showing whether they have stated a position. A candidate is not being asked to join something. They are being shown a gap in a public record that their opponents may fill.

Every figure then receives the same twenty-one statements that citizens answer. Their responses are published. So is their non-response.

This is the design choice that makes the register safe and makes it bite. The platform never characterises anyone's politics. It records whether a person seeking public office was willing to state a position when asked. "Invited on this date, did not respond" is a fact about them, not a claim by us — and the cost of silence becomes visible.

Where a figure does not respond, positions may be published only from verbatim documented statements or recorded votes, with source and date. Never a paraphrase. Never an inference from party membership.

Three layers, in order of cost

  • Stated positions. The twenty-one items, answered by them or quoted from the record. Immediate, and it pairs directly with a citizen's own result.
  • Documented record. Votes, budget lines, bills signed, appointments made. Harder, and this is where track record starts to bite.
  • Promises kept. Did the stated thing happen. The version that matters most and a standing editorial operation. Design for it now; staff it later.

The doctrines register

A separate section covers people who shape Nigerian economic thinking without seeking office — Africapitalism, industrial nationalism, developmental statism, Pan-Africanism. The unit there is a published doctrine, argued repeatedly and in writing, not a campaign promise. There is no non-response mechanism, because they are not asking for your vote. The standard is strictly their own published words.

Institutional requirements

A register that tracks promises and publishes non-response is an accountability instrument, and accountability instruments attract pressure and litigation. The protections are verbatim-only sourcing, published right of reply, a named editorial board rather than a single person, institutional backing from a university or established civic organisation, and legal review before launch. This is an argument for building it with a partner, not for softening it.
